# Assessment Marking Criteria — Legal Studies

## Overview

This document sets out the marking criteria for legal studies assessments. Marks are awarded according to the grade bands and criteria below. All marks are expressed as percentages.

---

## Grade Bands

| Grade | Mark Range | Classification |
|-------|-----------|----------------|
| A* | 90–100% | Exceptional / Outstanding First |
| A | 70–89% | First Class |
| B | 60–69% | Upper Second Class (2:1) |
| C | 50–59% | Lower Second Class (2:2) |
| D | 40–49% | Third Class |
| F | 0–39% | Fail |

---

## Marking Criteria

### 1. Knowledge and Understanding of the Law (30%)

| Mark Band | Descriptor |
|-----------|------------|
| 90–100% | Comprehensive and accurate knowledge of all relevant legal rules, principles, cases, and statutes. Demonstrates deep understanding of the theoretical and doctrinal foundations of the law. All legal propositions are correct and fully supported by authority. |
| 70–89% | Thorough and accurate knowledge of the relevant law. Legal principles, cases, and statutes are correctly identified and explained. Minor gaps or imprecision do not materially affect the quality of the answer. |
| 60–69% | Good knowledge of the main legal rules and principles. Most relevant cases and statutes are correctly identified. Some minor inaccuracies or omissions, but the overall legal framework is sound. |
| 50–59% | Adequate knowledge of the basic legal rules. Key cases and statutes are identified, though some important authorities may be missing or inaccurately stated. |
| 40–49% | Limited knowledge of the relevant law. Some correct points are made but there are significant gaps, inaccuracies, or misunderstandings of fundamental legal principles. |
| 0–39% | Insufficient or substantially incorrect knowledge. The legal framework is misunderstood or absent. Irrelevant material predominates. |

---

### 2. Analysis and Critical Evaluation (30%)

| Mark Band | Descriptor |
|-----------|------------|
| 90–100% | Exceptional critical analysis. Arguments are sophisticated, nuanced, and original. Competing perspectives (judicial, academic, policy) are fully engaged with. The student demonstrates the ability to evaluate conflicting authorities and scholarly debate at an advanced level. |
| 70–89% | Strong analytical ability. Legal issues are identified and explored in depth. Arguments are well-reasoned and show engagement with competing views, academic commentary, and policy considerations. Conclusions are clearly supported. |
| 60–69% | Good analysis of the main legal issues. The student goes beyond description and engages with arguments and counter-arguments. Some engagement with academic or policy perspectives. |
| 50–59% | Adequate analysis. The student identifies the main issues and makes some evaluative points but the analysis tends to be descriptive rather than genuinely critical. |
| 40–49% | Limited analysis. The answer is predominantly descriptive with only superficial evaluation. Issues may be identified but not properly explored. |
| 0–39% | Little or no meaningful analysis. The answer is descriptive, factually incorrect, or does not engage with the legal issues. |

---

### 3. Application of Law to Facts (25%)

| Mark Band | Descriptor |
|-----------|------------|
| 90–100% | Precise and sophisticated application of legal principles to the specific facts. All relevant issues are identified and resolved using correct legal reasoning. Cases are distinguished and analogised convincingly. Conclusions are clear, confident, and well-justified. |
| 70–89% | Accurate and detailed application of the law to the facts. The relevant legal tests are correctly applied. Cases are appropriately used and distinguished. Conclusions are clear and well-reasoned. |
| 60–69% | Good application of the law to the facts. Main issues are correctly resolved. Some relevant cases are applied, though the analysis of the facts may lack precision in places. |
| 50–59% | Adequate application. The student applies the law to the facts but may miss some issues or apply legal rules imprecisely. Conclusions are reached but may not be fully supported. |
| 40–49% | Limited application. The law is stated but not properly applied to the facts, or issues are identified but not resolved. Legal reasoning is incomplete or flawed. |
| 0–39% | Failure to apply the law to the facts. The answer states law in the abstract or applies it incorrectly. No meaningful conclusions are drawn. |

---

### 4. Structure, Clarity, and Presentation (10%)

| Mark Band | Descriptor |
|-----------|------------|
| 90–100% | Exceptionally well-structured response. Arguments flow logically and coherently. Headings and signposting used effectively. Writing is precise, concise, and professional in register. No grammatical or spelling errors. |
| 70–89% | Well-structured and clearly written. The answer is logically organised and easy to follow. Legal writing style is appropriate. Very few grammatical or presentational errors. |
| 60–69% | Generally clear structure. The answer is organised and mostly easy to follow. A few presentational issues but these do not impede understanding. |
| 50–59% | Adequate structure. The answer has a basic organisation but may lack clear signposting or logical flow in places. |
| 40–49% | Weak structure. The answer is difficult to follow in places. Ideas are not clearly organised and the writing is unclear. |
| 0–39% | No discernible structure. The answer is disorganised, unclear, or incoherent. |

---

### 5. Use of Legal Authority and Referencing (5%)

| Mark Band | Descriptor |
|-----------|------------|
| 90–100% | All legal propositions are supported by accurate citations. Cases and statutes are cited in the correct format (e.g., OSCOLA). No citation errors. |
| 70–89% | Legal propositions are consistently supported by accurate citations. Very minor citation errors only. |
| 60–69% | Most legal propositions are supported by citations. Some minor citation errors or inconsistencies in format. |
| 50–59% | Citations are provided for some legal propositions. Notable gaps or citation errors. |
| 40–49% | Few citations provided. Legal propositions are frequently unsupported by authority. |
| 0–39% | Citations absent or substantially incorrect throughout. |

---

## Summary Weighting

| Criterion | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Knowledge and Understanding of the Law | 30% |
| Analysis and Critical Evaluation | 30% |
| Application of Law to Facts | 25% |
| Structure, Clarity, and Presentation | 10% |
| Use of Legal Authority and Referencing | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

---

## General Guidance for Markers

- Marks should be awarded for the quality of the overall response, not for the number of points made.
- A response may perform differently across the criteria; the overall mark should reflect the weight given to each criterion.
- Where the law is unsettled, credit should be given for identifying and engaging with the uncertainty rather than asserting a false certainty.
- Markers should apply the criteria consistently and in accordance with the relevant grade band descriptors.
- All marks should be moderated in accordance with the department's quality assurance procedures.
